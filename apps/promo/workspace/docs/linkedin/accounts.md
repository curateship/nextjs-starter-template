# LinkedIn accounts

## Adding one

Accounts dashboard → Add account → LinkedIn. Tyler, 22 Oct 2026: both from
the start, so a LinkedIn account runs in a browser profile or on a rack phone,
picked when it is added. An important account runs on a phone only. A
profile that is the reader, or that searches X or LinkedIn, is never offered.

Sign it in once, in its browser window on the Browser profiles dashboard, or
in the LinkedIn app through the phone's screen window. Check now on the
Accounts dashboard reads who a browser account is signed in as, and takes the
weekly comments look. An account is named by its own page's address,
`in/sam-lee`. A look that sees nobody signed in, such as the code page or a
restriction screen, keeps the account's name and the day Promo first saw it,
so its warm-up does not start again; only a different member signing in
starts it again.

## The emailed code

LinkedIn emails a code almost every time an account signs in on a new device.
When a look finds LinkedIn asking for it, the bell rings once and the
account's settings window on the Accounts dashboard shows a box for it. Paste
the code there within 15 minutes and Promo types it into that account's
browser. The
code rides on its job only, and is wiped from the job the moment it is typed
or refused. A code older than 15 minutes is refused with a sentence to ask
LinkedIn for a new one. On a phone, type the code through the phone's screen
window instead.

Asking for the code is not a stop: the account is not marked blocked for it.

## Restriction screens

A "your account has been restricted", "temporarily restricted", "verify your
identity" or security check screen stops the account at once, in a browser
or on a phone. Promo records LinkedIn's own words, drops the account's
waiting jobs and marks their comments and posts failed with the reason, and
rings the bell once. Nothing goes out as that account until a person lets it
run again from the Accounts dashboard, whatever a later look says.

## Warm-up

Tyler, 22 Oct 2026: 7 days. LinkedIn shows nobody when an account joined, so
the days count from when Promo first saw it signed in. In those days it only
reads: once a workday it visits the home feed and searches a word or two of
its project's, and nothing is reacted to, connected or written. The Accounts
dashboard shows it as Warming up.

## Workday hours

Tyler, 22 Oct 2026: "Weekdays, 8am to 6pm", on the account's own clock, which
is the clock its browser or phone shows. A LinkedIn account does nothing of
its own outside them, reads included: comments, posts, connects and accepts
wait for the next weekday morning, and the line under Post says when. This
replaces the waking hours in Settings → Behaviour for LinkedIn. He picked
these fixed hours over a setting, so Settings → Behaviour shows them as a
line, not as a field.

## Premium

The account's Settings window has a Premium switch, also read off the gold
badge when Promo looks at who is signed in. Premium shows every profile
viewer and every number; a free account's numbers that only Premium sees
stay blank, never 0.

## Its numbers over time

Once a week the account's own browser reads its followers, connections,
profile views and post impressions off its dashboard, and the Settings window
draws them week by week. A phone account has no weekly reading, because the
app does not show the dashboard.

## Important and throwaway accounts

The rules every network has, plus LinkedIn's own: a throwaway never comments
on, reacts to, reposts or tags an important account's post or page, and the
two groups never connect with each other. Two of the person's accounts never
comment on one post at all.

## Moving to a phone

A LinkedIn account on a browser profile moves to a rack phone the way Reddit
and X accounts do, from its settings under Throwaway. Sign in to the LinkedIn
app on the phone through its screen window; the move finishes when the phone
reads the account's own page address. LinkedIn emails a code on the new
device, which is typed on the phone by hand.

## Messages

A LinkedIn account in a browser has its messages read every three hours in
its workday, by the Messages dashboard's own read and answer jobs, and named
by the conversation and the other person's page. On a phone they come in on
the app's notifications, as every phone account's do. Send answers only that
conversation, to only that person.
